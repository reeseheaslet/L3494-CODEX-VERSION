Overtime scenarios
1. Forced on a 96- personnel can be forced on a 96 – However, if the spot
being filled is not the next day, the person that is up to be forced is “on the
hook” for the spot, but not put in the schedule. The department will try to find
another person to force who will not be put on a 96. If nobody meets that
criteria before the start of that shift, the person who is “on the hook” will be
forced. If the spot is for the next day, then that person would be forced on a
96,

2. Bereavement leave does not protect you from being forced on the in-
between days of your set—unless on that date of the potential forcing is a
date where you are doing the bereavement related event. (For example a
funeral for the deceased lands on a day you don’t work the department would
not force you for that day). It is treated the same as if you are sick: if you are
sick and cannot come into work due to being unable to perform the job the
department would not force you to come in and work.

3. In a situation where a driver took vacation and the other on duty FF at that
station (31 or 32) is not a driver, you call specifically for engine drivers. Even
if at a different station there are 2 engine driver qualified firefighters. If no
engine driver takes it you then can move an engine driver and call for FF1
firefighters to avoid forcing. Basically the idea is we don’t make firefighters
move stations just because a FF1 is boxes back in the recall book. This does
not apply to station 33 with the truck. We do bump FF2s from the truck to
engine 33 driver to call for FF1 (tiller qualified) drivers if those FF1s are boxes
back.

4. When filling for OT when somebody is returning from injury leave. It is case
by case: if the person returning is confident they will be cleared they can be
called for OT on dates that are after their appointment, even if the call for OT
is being made before the appointment. If this is not the case then that person
won’t be called. Check with BC to clarify if said person should be included on
the OT recall list. The idea being that we try to anticipate the most likely
outcome, and fill OT for that.

5. Trades - treat “apparatus operator” as somebody that is qualified to drive
ALL apparatus (in most cases a FFII). An apparatus operator can trade with a
non-apparatus operator if said apparatus operator is not scheduled to drive
that day. If he becomes scheduled to drive and that trade causes overtime
then the trade will be cancelled. Non ‘apparatus operators’ can trade with
non apparatus operators and when that trade causes overtime due to the
TRADE OFF person being qualified to drive a specific spot (tiller/squad/etc)
that the TRADE ON person is not qualified to drive - then overtime will be
called and the trade is kept.

6. If a spot becomes vacant during a shift which causes the dept to dip below
minimum staffing and is greater than 12 hours OT. Then emergency recall
will be initiated (to try to get the spot filled ASAP) and standard recall
procedures will be started to fill for the rest of the shift.
7. Out of rank overtime. This is reserved for special circumstances that
requires the fire chief’s approval.

8. When filling vacancies with recalled FFs and Captains, the goal is always
to determine what “caused” the vacancy and to fill with a like position. In
other words, if a Captain “caused” the vacancy, it should be filled with a
Captain. Similarly, if a FF “caused” the vacancy, it should be filled with a FF
(making sure that they are qualified as a driver, squad driver, etc. if
necessary).

9. If somebody who accepted OT, and is slotted in to work OT in the schedule,
gets moved off the schedule in a way that would normally “cause” overtime –
that open spot gets filled with whatever originally caused that OT. Example:
an open spot is caused by a FF, but due to a captain vacation there is only an
empty slot for a captain. You bump up an AC and call for a FF. Let’s say no
FFs take the OT, and you bump the AC back down and fill the spot with a
Captain to avoid forcing. Then the next day that captain gets sent out as a
strike team leader trainee. You would revert back to calling FFs for that spot
again first, and ultimately would force a FF if needed for that spot. That spot
is not “caused” by a Captain because somebody cannot “cause” a spot if it is
not their shift.
